RESPIRATORY ANATOMY — BOUNDED ELEVEN-DECK COVERAGE AUDIT
Completed 2026-08-28. Local source material only; no internet, transcripts, audio, past papers, Sites tools, or site edits.

RESULT
Eight justified additions are in anatomy-additions.jsonl (resp-anat-049 through resp-anat-056).
The original 48-question payload, image catalog, raw images and original audit are unchanged.
If integrated, the combined text set has 56 questions. The supplement adds no image files.

METHOD
All eleven local respiratory Anatomy decks were compared with the existing 48 questions using their embedded text and rendered slide OCR. Full-slide visual checks were performed for each slide supporting a new item, including its diagrams, annotations and attribution. Relevant Gray's Anatomy for Students 3rd-edition sections were used to verify wording and precise chapter/page locators. This is a slide-based audit, not a claim that every fact the lecturer may have spoken is represented; audio/transcripts were out of scope.

SPECIFIC REQUESTED CHECKS

Diaphragm and intercostal respiratory mechanics:
The diaphragm and intercostal muscles appear as anatomical landmarks in Trachea and lung 1 slide 9. Trachea and lung 3 slide 8 explicitly shows C3-C5 roots, both phrenic nerves, and their branches to the diaphragm's inferior surface: this supports addition 050, including the corresponding Gray's p162 explanation of dome flattening.
No dedicated pump-handle/bucket-handle, intercostal fiber/action, or rib-movement mechanics teaching was found in the eleven respiratory Anatomy decks. These topics ARE in the allowed Gray's book (printed pp150-154 and 162-163), but a book containing a topic does not establish lecturer emphasis for this deck set. They were not added as new slide-grounded examination objectives. They may belong with cardiovascular thoracic-wall anatomy or respiratory physiology, or may have been taught verbally; this audit cannot settle that without the excluded audio/transcripts.

Pleural drainage:
Existing 043 covers the costodiaphragmatic recess and fluid accumulation, and 044 covers inferior lung/pleural levels. The decks show recesses and pleural projections repeatedly, but no dedicated thoracentesis/drain-placement procedure or complete parietal-pleural fluid-resorption pathway was found. Do not conflate the added pulmonary lymph route with a verified account of every pleural-fluid drainage mechanism.

Lymphatics:
There are explicit, previously untested slides on pulmonary (trachea and lung 3, 7/9), nasal (Nasal Bones 4, 7), and laryngeal (LARYNX 4, 11) lymphatics. Those are addressed by 049, 054 and 056. The sources support the selected regional routes; unnecessary precise clinical nodal-level claims and oversimplified statements about vocal-fold lymphatics were not introduced.
