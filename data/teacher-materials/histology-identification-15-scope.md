# Aug 22 histology practical — 15-slide identification mode

## Confirmed assessment format

The class clarification says this is not a typical theory exam. Students must
differentiate the 15 revision slides, view specimens under the microscope and
write what they see. The dedicated `histo-identification-15` collection is
therefore restricted to specimen identification and identification of visible
parts. It contains no standalone mechanism or physiology questions.

The focused bank is delivered as written recall rather than visible multiple
choice. Every specimen-recognition item presents a wide architectural field
beside a diagnostic close field before the student types the tissue name.

## Required specimens

1. Trachea
2. Urinary bladder
3. Spongy (trabecular) bone
4. Hyaline cartilage
5. Synovial joint
6. Peripheral nerve
7. Sensory ganglion
8. Skin with hair (thin skin)
9. Skin without hair (thick/glabrous skin)
10. White adipose tissue
11. Brown adipose tissue
12. Thyroid gland
13. Skeletal muscle
14. Cardiac muscle
15. Tendon

## Corrections and trust policy

- The later group clarification explicitly identifies the revision bone slide as
  **spongy bone with trabeculae**, not compact bone.
- Student captions, AI guesses and answer overlays are treated as untrusted.
  Tissue identity and part labels are keyed from morphology and cross-checked
  against Junqueira's Basic Histology (16th ed.), diFiore's Atlas of Histology
  (11th ed.) and the supplied PRACTICAL.pptx.
- The group warned that hair-bearing versus hairless-skin markings conflicted.
  Those labels were not copied; the bank uses follicles/sebaceous glands for
  hair-bearing skin and a thick stratum corneum/eccrine glands with no follicles
  for glabrous skin.
- The 2026-08-21 priority announcement is exhaustively represented as six
  discrimination groups: joint/cartilage/bone; ganglion/nerve;
  cardiac/skeletal muscle; white/brown adipose/tendon; thin/thick skin; and
  simple cuboidal/transitional/pseudostratified epithelium. The epithelial
  group is trained through thyroid, bladder and trachea respectively.

## Image provenance and privacy

- At least three answer-neutral fields are available for every specimen from
  verified local, textbook, deck and course assets.
- The complete 14-photo course practical album, its separate trachea photo, two
  detailed nerve photos and later raw bone/cartilage originals were recovered
  at full resolution. Twelve specimen overviews and two second-field examples
  were accepted after independent morphology review.
- The album entries captioned brown fat, ganglion and cardiac muscle were not
  used because their raw morphology did not independently establish those
  identities. Brown fat and cardiac muscle use verified local/textbook fields;
  sensory ganglion uses answer-neutral crops from open teaching micrographs by
  Merlin-UK on Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 3.0) and UNSW Embryology.
- The extra low-power longitudinal course photo was retained in the private raw
  download set but excluded from questions because it could be confused with
  tendon without higher-power nerve landmarks.
- Every committed course image is a tight microscope-field crop. Student or AI
  labels, sender names, phone numbers and chat UI are excluded.
- Raw WhatsApp downloads remain private and are not committed. Supplying their
  temporary parent directory through `WHATSAPP_PRACTICAL_DIR` reproduces only
  the accepted crops; the older `WHATSAPP_PRACTICAL_SCREENSHOT` input remains a
  limited fallback for the original preview crop.

## Bank shape

The collection contains 60 written image questions: four per specimen. Every
specimen has two paired wide/close recognition drills plus two marker-based
part-identification questions. Hidden comparison keys retain explicit reasoning
for the correct diagnosis and three high-yield look-alikes; they appear only
after the student submits a typed answer and also ground optional Codex grading.
