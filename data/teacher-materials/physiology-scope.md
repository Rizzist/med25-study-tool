## Bottom line

The teacher material defines three connected exam domains:

1. **Cardiac physiology and ECG** — action potentials, pacemakers, excitation-contraction coupling, cardiac cycle, pressure-volume mechanics, conduction, leads, rate, rhythm, axis, and ECG image interpretation.
2. **Circulatory physiology/hemodynamics** — viscosity, pressure-flow-resistance relations, vessel mechanics, pressure measurement, capillary exchange, edema, venous return, local/central regulation, special circulations, exercise, coronary flow, and hemorrhage/shock.
3. **Blood physiology** — erythrocytes and anemia/polycythemia, platelets/coagulation/fibrinolysis, and leukocytes/inflammation.

The scope is therefore much broader than cardiac physiology alone, but it is still a coherent **cardiovascular and blood** block. A provisional general-physiology bank based only on introductory Guyton chapters should remain separate.

## Detailed scope map

### 1. Cardiac electrical physiology — very high priority

- Fast-response action-potential phases 0–4 and fast Na+, L/T-type Ca2+, `Ito`, `IKr`, `IKs`, and `IK1` currents.
- Slow-response/pacemaker potentials, funny current (`If`), and differences from contractile cells.
- Effective versus relative refractory periods and restoration of ionic gradients.
- Sympathetic beta-1/cAMP and parasympathetic M2 effects; chronotropy, dromotropy, inotropy, lusitropy, and bathmotropy.

Evidence: `Cardiac action potential.ppt`; `Cardiac Physiology 1.ppt`.

### 2. Cardiac muscle, cycle, and ventricular mechanics — very high priority

- Cardiac muscle structure, intercalated discs, T-tubules, and functional chamber/circuit organization.
- Calcium-induced calcium release through L-type channels, ryanodine receptors, and SR Ca2+ sparks.
- Ventricular filling, diastasis, atrial systole, isovolumetric contraction, ejection, isovolumetric relaxation, valve events, and S1.
- Pressure-volume loops, EDV/ESV, stroke volume, external work, preload, afterload, and contractility.
- Length-tension/Frank-Starling behavior, Laplace law, concentric/eccentric hypertrophy, myocardial oxygen demand, and neural regulation.

Evidence: `Cardiac Physiology 1.ppt`; `Cardiac physiology 2.pptx`.

### 3. Rhythmical excitation, ECG, and vector analysis — very high priority

- Pacemaker/conduction pathways and atrial-ventricular coordination.
- P, QRS, T, PR, QT, and ST; depolarization/repolarization sequence.
- Einthoven triangle; standard and augmented limb leads; unipolar/bipolar logic.
- Regular and irregular heart-rate calculation and normal sinus-rhythm criteria.
- Mean QRS axis, left/right deviation, quadrant method, and equiphasic-lead method.
- V1–V6 positions, limb-electrode placement, ST/T changes, and myocardial territory localization.
- Assignment tasks: recognize a rhythm, identify an ECG setting adjustment, count heart rate, and determine axis from an equiphasic lead.

Evidence: `Rythmical Excitation.ppt`; `ECG  & vectorial analysis.pptx`; `ECG 1.ppt`; `ECG 2.ppt`; `ECG Assignment.ppt`.

### 4. Circulation foundations and blood rheology — high priority

- Purposes of circulation: nutrient/O2 delivery, waste clearance, hormonal distribution, temperature regulation, immunity, and homeostasis.
- Systemic/pulmonary and series/parallel organization; consequences for flow, pressure, and resistance.
- Newtonian versus non-Newtonian fluids, viscosity versus fluidity, and the dominant RBC contribution to blood viscosity.
- Effects of polycythemia, smoking/altitude, and anemia on viscosity and cardiac workload.

Evidence: `Cairculatory System 1.pptx` (29 slides).

### 5. Flow, resistance, vessel mechanics, and measurement — very high priority

- Hooke/spring behavior and vascular elasticity.
- Laplace relation among pressure, radius, and wall tension.
- Circulatory Ohm relation: pressure difference, flow, and resistance.
- Poiseuille determinants: radius, length, and viscosity; fourth-power sensitivity of flow to radius.
- Resistance versus conductance and peripheral resistance units.
- Velocity versus flow and cross-sectional area.
- Electromagnetic, Doppler ultrasound, and laser-Doppler flow measurement; generator/right-hand rule concept.

Evidence: `Cairculatory System 2.pptx` (17 slides).

### 6. Pressure, compliance, pulse, and flow pattern — very high priority

- Static/filling versus dynamic pressure; hydrostatic and kinetic components; Bernoulli effect in narrowing.
- Systolic and diastolic pressure, isovolumetric contraction/relaxation, and pulse pressure.
- Arterial versus venous capacity, capacitance, and compliance.
- Systemic versus pulmonary resistance and pressure-flow relations.
- Laminar versus turbulent flow and Reynolds-number concept.
- Direct versus indirect pressure measurement, palpation/auscultation, Korotkoff phases, and pulse-wave versus blood velocity.

Evidence: `Cairculatory System 3.pptx` (18 slides).

### 7. Microcirculation, capillary exchange, edema, and veins — very high priority

- Distribution of blood volume and velocity across arteries, capillaries, veins, lungs, and heart.
- Continuous/tight, regular, fenestrated, and discontinuous capillary pathways; diffusion, pores, and transcytosis.
- Hydrostatic and colloid-osmotic forces, plasma-protein contributions, Gibbs-Donnan effect, filtration coefficient, and lymph flow.
- Mechanisms of edema.
- Gravity, hydrostatic columns, venous valves, skeletal-muscle pump, and pressure above/below the heart.
- Active and reactive hyperemia.

Evidence: `Cairculatory System 4.pptx` (21 slides).

### 8. Local, humoral, neural, and reflex control — very high priority

- Metabolic/local regulation by tissue O2 and nutrient need, vasodilator metabolites, NO, endothelin, and myogenic responses.
- Vasoconstrictors: angiotensin II, vasopressin/ADH, catecholamines, endothelin; vasodilators: bradykinin, histamine, NO, and related peptides.
- Vasomotor-center excitatory/inhibitory regions, sympathetic tone, vagal cardioinhibition, hypothalamus, and motor cortex.
- Baroreceptor and chemoreceptor anatomy, afferents (Hering/glossopharyngeal and vagal), medullary integration, and negative feedback.
- Atrial stretch/Bainbridge-volume reflexes, ANP, ADH, renal perfusion, and urine/salt-water responses.

Evidence: `Cairculatory System 4.pptx`; `Cairculatory System 5.pptx`.

### 9. Special circulations, exercise, coronary flow, and hemorrhage — high priority

- Brain, heart/coronary, liver, skeletal muscle, and pulmonary flows and their characteristic regulation.
- Exercise hyperemia, local hypoxia, mass sympathetic discharge, redistribution away from kidney/GI, venoconstriction, increased filling pressure, cardiac output, and arterial pressure.
- Phasic coronary flow, myocardial compression, metabolic/O2 control, and autonomic influences.
- Compensatory response to hemorrhage: reduced baroreceptor firing, vasomotor activation, tachycardia, renal/GI/skin/muscle vasoconstriction, oliguria, and reversible versus irreversible shock.

Evidence: `Cairculatory System 5.pptx`; `Cairculatory System 6.pptx`.

### 10. Red blood cells — very high priority

- RBC structure, deformability, count, hematocrit, hemoglobin, carbonic anhydrase, and buffering.
- Hematopoiesis/erythropoiesis stages and marrow distribution with age.
- Tissue hypoxia, renal erythropoietin, HIF-1, and the erythropoietic response timeline.
- Vitamin B12/folate maturation, hemoglobin synthesis, sickle hemoglobin, and O2 binding.
- Iron absorption, transferrin, ferritin, distribution, recycling, RBC lifespan, and macrophage disposal.
- Morphologic classifications and major anemia categories; secondary polycythemia versus polycythemia vera and viscosity.

Evidence: `blood 1.ppsx` (33 slides).

### 11. Hemostasis, coagulation, and fibrinolysis — very high priority

- Vascular spasm, platelet plug, coagulation, clot retraction, organization, and dissolution.
- Platelet formation, structure, lifespan, adhesion through collagen/vWF, activation, ADP/thromboxane A2, and aggregation.
- Serum versus plasma and vascular-platelet versus coagulation hemostasis.
- Clotting factors and the prothrombin-activator, thrombin, fibrin sequence.
- Intrinsic and extrinsic pathways and their simultaneous interaction after vessel injury.
- Roles of Ca2+, vitamin K, liver, platelet phospholipid surfaces, proteins C/S, and bile-dependent vitamin-K absorption.
- Plasminogen, tPA, plasmin, fibrinolysis, and removal of unnecessary microclots.

Evidence: `blood 2.ppsx` (32 slides).

### 12. White blood cells and inflammation — very high priority

- WBC types, normal differential/count, marrow versus lymphoid origins, storage, and lifespans.
- Granulocyte/monocyte maturation and lymphocyte/plasma-cell production sites.
- Diapedesis, amoeboid movement, chemotaxis, opsonization, and phagocytosis.
- Neutrophil versus macrophage capacity; lysosomal enzymes, oxidative killing, myeloperoxidase/hypochlorite, and macrophage lipases.
- Monocyte-macrophage/reticuloendothelial system: skin, nodes, lungs, liver/Kupffer cells, spleen, and marrow.
- Inflammation: vasodilation, permeability, local clotting, leukocyte migration, swelling, walling-off, defense sequence, cytokine/CSF feedback, and pus.
- Eosinophils in parasites/allergy, basophils/mast-cell-like mediators and IgE, leukopenia, and myelogenous/lymphogenous leukemia.
