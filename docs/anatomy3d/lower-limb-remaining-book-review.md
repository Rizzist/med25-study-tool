# Lower-limb practical: remaining book review

This checklist covers the audit's **42 reference-only targets and nine context-only targets**. It is not an official examination list or a ranking of likely questions. Use your course list to decide what must be identified on a bone, specimen or prosection.

For each item: orient the side and view, point to the structure, then distinguish it from the nearest similar structure. **Context** means the gallery shows an attachment site, network or region, not an exact whole-structure target. PDF page numbers below are viewer page numbers in the local Gray's Anatomy, 42nd edition; printed pages are given where useful.

## Bones

- [ ] Arcuate line — chapter 77, PDF 2138–2139 (printed 1357–1358).
- [ ] Pubic crest and ischial ramus — chapter 77, PDF 2138 and 2140 (printed 1357 and 1359). Keep the pubic crest distinct from the pubic tubercle.
- [ ] Anterior intercondylar area and posterior intercondylar area of tibia — chapter 78, PDF 2204 (printed 1405). Do not substitute the intercondylar eminence or one ligament attachment for the whole area.
- [ ] Fibular notch of tibia and malleolar fossa of fibula — chapter 78, PDF 2198–2199 (printed 1400–1401).
- [ ] Lateral process of talus and groove for flexor hallucis longus on talus — chapter 79, talus/body section, PDF 2235 (printed 1434). Section-review locator: the audit did not establish exact native callouts for these two names. A generic ankle groove label does not prove a talus-specific location.
- [ ] First metatarsal: shaft and head; second, third, fourth and fifth metatarsals: base, shaft and head — chapter 79, PDF 2237–2238, figures 79.5–79.6. These are 14 remaining subregion targets; identify each individual bone before its subregion. The first metatarsal base already has a named gallery target.

## Ligaments and menisci

- [ ] Transverse acetabular ligament — chapter 77, PDF 2134 and 2153 (printed 1354 and 1368).
- [ ] Anterior meniscofemoral ligament — chapter 78, PDF 2205 (printed 1406).
- [ ] Meniscotibial ligaments — chapter 78, PDF 2206 (printed 1407). Section-review locator; the earlier exact-name match was unresolved. Do not equate a whole collateral ligament with its meniscotibial component.
- [ ] Anterior tibiotalar part of deltoid ligament — chapter 79, PDF 2251 (printed 1445). Distinguish this part from the separately labeled posterior tibiotalar part.
- [ ] Lisfranc ligament — chapter 79, PDF 2275 (printed 1464.e1).
- [ ] Interphalangeal collateral ligaments and plantar plates — chapter 79, PDF 2262 and 2264 (printed 1453–1454). The interphalangeal item is a section-review locator. Metatarsophalangeal collateral callouts do not identify interphalangeal collaterals; generic plantar-ligament labels are not proof of a separately delineated plantar plate.
- [ ] **Context:** medial meniscus anterior horn, medial meniscus posterior horn, lateral meniscus anterior horn and lateral meniscus posterior horn — chapter 78, PDF 2203–2205 (printed 1404–1406). Figure 78.13 identifies their tibial attachment sites: medial anterior 5, medial posterior 9, lateral anterior 2, lateral posterior 4. Review the menisci themselves separately; an attachment area on dry bone is not an in-situ horn surface.

## Tendons and fascial structures

- [ ] Sartorius tendon and gracilis tendon — chapter 78, PDF 2204, figure 78.14B; supplementary regional review in chapter 76, PDF 2102 (printed 1335). Trace the tendons separately rather than answering with the muscle group.
- [ ] Iliopsoas tendon — chapter 77, PDF 2133–2134 (printed 1353.e1–1354).
- [ ] Femoral sheath — chapter 77, PDF 2131–2132 and 2171 (printed 1352–1353 and 1381).
- [ ] Fibrous flexor sheaths of toes and extensor expansions of toes — chapter 79, PDF 2262 and 2268–2269 (printed 1453 and 1458–1459). Distinguish a sheath or expansion from the tendon passing through or joining it.

## Nerves

- [ ] Fibular communicating branch — chapters 76 and 78. Exact-name locator remains unresolved; section entry points are PDF 2098 (chapter 76) and PDF 2192 (chapter 78). Confirm terminology against the book/course specimen rather than assuming the nearby sural branch is equivalent.
- [ ] Lateral calcaneal branches — chapter 78, PDF 2227 (printed 1428).
- [ ] Inferior calcaneal nerve (Baxter nerve) — chapter 79, PDF 2272 (printed 1462). Do not replace it with the medial calcaneal branches.
- [ ] Dorsal digital nerves — chapter 78, PDF 2228 (printed 1429), and chapter 79, PDF 2250 (printed 1444).
- [ ] **Context:** lumbar plexus — chapter 61, PDF 1803–1804 (printed 1146–1147), figures 61.15–61.16. Review roots and named branches as a network; there is no fabricated single plexus hotspot.
- [ ] **Context:** sacral plexus — chapter 71, PDF 1975 (printed 1252), figure 71.10. Follow the lumbosacral trunk and named branches in their pelvic relationships.

## Muscles

- [ ] Hamstring part of adductor magnus — chapter 77, PDF 2144–2145 (printed 1362–1363), with the posterior femoral attachment review. Whole adductor magnus selection does not separately identify this part.
- [ ] **Context:** articularis genus — chapter 77, PDF 2143 (printed 1361), figure 77.11B, number 10; further text locator PDF 2175 (printed 1383). The figure identifies attachment sites on femur, not a separately dissected muscle belly.

## Regions and their contents

- [ ] Adductor canal — chapter 77, PDF 2171 (printed 1381); chapter 76, PDF 2107 and 2117 (printed 1337 and 1344). Review its boundaries and contents on a specimen, not just the name of a vessel passing through it.
- [ ] **Context:** femoral triangle — chapter 77, PDF 2171, figure 77.44. Identify its boundaries and individual contents; the region is not one solid structure.
- [ ] **Context:** tarsal tunnel — chapter 79, PDF 2252 (printed 1446), figure 79.15, and PDF 2272 (printed 1462). The transverse ankle image supports relationships, not a separately marked tunnel boundary.

Source basis: the existing source/coverage audit only; no new anatomy claims, images or examination priorities were added in this checklist. A reference-only status means there is no exact interactive gallery target in the audited app, not that the structure is absent from the textbook.
