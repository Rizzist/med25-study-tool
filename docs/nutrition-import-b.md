## Corrections and fairness

- DDS Q6: K1/phytonadione, not the marked K3/menadione. The item identifies the vitamin form, not a complete neonatal bleeding protocol.
- DDS Q28: total skeletal-muscle glycogen, not the marked liver answer (which confuses concentration with total quantity).
- DDS Q17 remains ungraded: maternal fat plus muscle versus fetus depends on the composition model; the local book’s example gives 7 lb for infant and 7 lb for fat stores alone.
- DDS Q30 remains ungraded: ordinary fat oxidation, nutritional ketosis and pathological ketoacidosis cannot be collapsed into a universal fetal-neurotoxicity claim.
- DDS Q24 accepts both mono- and polyunsaturated fats. DDS Q32 accepts excess vitamin A and low protein as plausible fracture-risk factors.
- February Q31: current AAP guidance supports avoiding fruit juice before 12 months; egg-white avoidance for the entire first year is outdated.
- February Q33: both ≤50% and ≤40% energy intake for at least five days satisfy the stated acute-malnutrition intake threshold. Both are accepted; intake alone is not a complete diagnosis.
- Explicit study repairs preserve `originalQuestion` for nonspecific or defective items. Examples: whole grains versus 4 kcal/g carbohydrate; GLUT5’s apical location versus GLUT2; thiamin toxicity absolutes; a supposedly ideal omega ratio; osteopenia falsely described as protected from vitamin D deficiency; FOS allegedly not affecting bowel movements; sodium allegedly never changing in refeeding; constipation falsely excluded as a tube-feeding complication.
- Infant supplementation is explicitly an Iranian source-course convention, not a universal prescription or all nutrients starting from birth.
- No source questions were omitted. Repairs are not to be represented as untouched historical stems, and should not inflate exact-repeat evidence.
