## Questions

### Q22 (PDF p1) · source page 1

All arteries carry blood:

A. To the liver
B. To the brain
C. Away from the lungs
D. Away from the heart

**Answer: D.** Arteries are defined by carrying blood away from the heart, not by oxygen content. Pulmonary arteries carry deoxygenated blood away from the right ventricle.
Printed Q22.

### Q23 (PDF p1) · source page 1

The arteries supplying the tissue of the heart are:

A. Renal arteries
B. Myocardial arteries
C. Coronary arteries
D. Venae cavae

**Answer: C.** Right and left coronary arteries supply the myocardium. Venae cavae are veins returning systemic blood to the right atrium.
Printed Q23.

### Q24 (PDF p1) · source page 1

Nervous control of the heart is exerted by:

A. Nerves from the thoracic region of the spinal column
B. The second and third cranial nerves
C. Fibers of the sensory somatic system
D. Fibers of the autonomic nervous system

**Answer: D.** The complete system is autonomic: sympathetic and parasympathetic efferents alter cardiac rate, conduction and force. A names only part of the sympathetic origin.
Printed Q24.

### Q26 (PDF p1) · source page 1

Vasoconstriction refers to:

A. Increasing vessel lumen size
B. Decreasing vessel lumen size
C. Delivering oxygen and nutrients
D. Delivering waste products to the kidney

**Answer: B.** Vascular smooth-muscle contraction reduces luminal radius and usually increases resistance; it does not name a transport function.
Printed Q26.

### Q28 (PDF p2) · source page 2

Which factor most strongly favors movement of fluid into capillary blood from interstitial fluid in the conventional Starling model?

A. Tissue hydrostatic pressure
B. Tissue osmotic pressure
C. Blood colloid osmotic pressure
D. Blood hydrostatic pressure

**Answer: C.** Plasma proteins, particularly albumin, generate plasma oncotic pressure, which opposes filtration. Modern glycocalyx-aware physiology does not assume sustained venous-end reabsorption in every systemic capillary.
Printed Q28. Conventional course force-balance model.

### Q32 (PDF p3) · source page 3

Which has a powerful role in lysis of a blood clot?

A. Plasmin
B. Trypsin
C. Fibrinogen
D. Vitamin K

**Answer: A.** Plasmin cleaves fibrin. Plasminogen is activated to plasmin by activators such as tPA; fibrinogen is the precursor of fibrin, and vitamin K supports synthesis of several coagulation factors.
Printed Q32.

### Q26 (PDF p5) · source page 5

During RBC differentiation, which cell retains remnants of organelles/RNA?

A. Proerythroblast
B. Basophilic erythroblast
C. Colony-forming-unit blast
D. Reticulocyte

**Answer: D.** The reticulocyte is an enucleated immature RBC with residual ribosomal RNA and organelle remnants that disappear as it matures.
Printed Q26; the source specifically mentions Golgi/mitochondrial remnants.

### Q27 (PDF p5) · source page 5

Which decreases renal erythropoietin production?

A. Pulmonary disease
B. Anemia
C. Tissue oxygenation
D. Chronic blood loss

**Answer: C.** Improved tissue oxygen availability reduces hypoxia-driven renal EPO signaling. The other choices generally increase EPO if they reduce oxygen delivery and renal EPO production is intact.
Printed Q27.

### Q28 (PDF p5) · source page 5

Which genotype produces neither A nor B red-cell agglutinogens?

A. OB
B. OA
C. OO
D. BB

**Answer: C.** An OO genotype gives group O, which lacks A and B antigens on red cells. It still has other membrane antigens, including H substance.
Printed Q28.

### Q29 (PDF p5) · source page 5

A pig heart valve transplanted into a human is a:

A. Xenograft
B. Allograft
C. Isograft
D. Autograft

**Answer: A.** A xenograft crosses species. An allograft is between genetically different members of one species, an isograft between genetically identical individuals, and an autograft within one person.
Printed Q29.

### Q30 (PDF p5) · source page 5

In a best-possible tissue-matched kidney transplant, which listed component is most associated with rejection?

A. Antibodies
B. Platelets
C. Rh antigens
D. T cells

**Answer: D.** T cells recognize donor antigens and drive major cellular rejection mechanisms even after good matching.
Printed Q30. Antibody-mediated rejection also exists; T cells are the intended general cellular-rejection answer, not an exclusive mechanism. The source's fanciful 240-year-old vignette is not a biological age assumption.

### Q31 (PDF p5) · source page 5

What is the rate-limiting step in the conventional blood-coagulation cascade?

A. Fibrin polymerization
B. Formation of prothrombin activator
C. Calcium-ion concentration
D. Thrombin concentration

**Answer: B.** The textbook cascade describes formation of the prothrombin-activator complex as the slow initiating step; subsequent thrombin generation and fibrin formation proceed rapidly.
Printed Q31, fourth option continues on PDF page 3. This is the simplified cascade model, not a full cell-based kinetics model.
