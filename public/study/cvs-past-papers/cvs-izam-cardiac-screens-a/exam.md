## Questions

### Q2 · source page 2

Which ion is responsible for fast-fiber depolarization?

A. Calcium
B. Potassium/calcium/sodium
C. Sodium
D. Potassium

**Answer: C.** Phase 0 of a working atrial/ventricular myocyte is produced mainly by rapid inward sodium current through fast voltage-gated sodium channels.


### Q4 · source page 6

Ventricular Purkinje-fiber impulse transmission velocity is:

A. Increased by parasympathetic stimulation
B. Decreased by parasympathetic stimulation
C. Not appreciably affected by parasympathetic stimulation
D. None of the above

**Answer: C.** In the basic course model, vagal effects concentrate in atrial and nodal tissue; they are not a major controller of ventricular Purkinje conduction.
This is the simplified teaching distinction, not a claim that all ventricular parasympathetic effects are impossible.

### Q6 · source page 7

At the beginning of diastole, ventricular blood volumes are approximately:

A. Both zero
B. 50 mL each
C. Different
D. 120 mL each

**Answer: B.** Diastole begins after ejection, so each ventricle contains its end-systolic volume, commonly approximated as 50 mL. About 120 mL is the usual end-diastolic teaching value.
Normal right/left volumes and ejection fractions need not be numerically identical; the question uses a simplified paired-ventricle model.

### Q10 · source page 8

In order to induce an action potential in slow fibers, which type of calcium channel is necessary?

A. Transient
B. Latent (slow)
C. Both A and B
D. Specific calcium channel

**Answer: B.** The slow-response nodal phase-0 upstroke is produced by L-type calcium current. T-type current can help late phase-4 depolarization but is not the principal current producing the action-potential upstroke.
The source says 'latent (slow)', interpreted as long-lasting L-type channels. Distinguish inducing phase 0 from currents contributing to pacemaker depolarization.

### Q7 · source page 9

Inhibition of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pump will:

A. Increase myocyte contraction force
B. Decrease myocyte contraction force
C. Inhibit the calcium pump directly
D. Have no effect on cardiac contractility

**Answer: A.** Pump inhibition raises intracellular sodium, reducing calcium extrusion through Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchange. More available calcium can increase contractility, the classic cardiac-glycoside mechanism.
Assumes a viable, ATP-supplied myocyte and a pharmacological range, not profound poisoning or ischemia.

### Q8 · source page 11

The calcium used for cardiac contraction comes:

A. Mostly from extracellular fluid
B. Only from inside the cell
C. Mostly from inside the cell
D. Only from outside the cell

**Answer: C.** Most activator calcium is released from the sarcoplasmic reticulum, triggered by a smaller but essential extracellular calcium influx. Neither source alone describes normal excitation–contraction coupling.


### Q1 · source page 12

AV delay duration is normally?

A. 0.09 seconds
B. 0.21 seconds
C. 0.03 seconds
D. 0.16 seconds

**Answer: A.** Guyton separates about 0.09 s in the AV node from additional penetrating-bundle delay. About 0.16 s is the broader atrial-to-ventricular timing, not the node-only component.
The original says 'AV delay'; the keyed interpretation is the AV-node component. Total AV junction delay is about 0.13 s and is not offered.

### Q9 · source page 13

Which listed leads best evaluate the left cardiac border?

A. II and III
B. I and aVL
C. V2 and V3
D. V4 and V5

**Answer: B.** I and aVL are the high-lateral limb leads. II/III are inferior and V2/V3 are predominantly septal/anterior. V4/V5 include anterior/lateral information but are not the best listed paired high-lateral leads.
‘Border’ is imprecise surface-anatomy wording; interpret it as high-lateral electrical territory.

### Q17 · source page 14

Which wave occurs during isovolumetric contraction?

A. Dicrotic notch
B. Atrial a wave
C. Atrial c wave
D. Atrial v wave

**Answer: C.** The c wave is produced partly by bulging of the closed AV valve into the atrium as ventricular pressure rises. The a wave reflects atrial systole; the arterial notch follows semilunar closure.


### Q11 · source page 15

The arterial (semilunar) valves close at:

A. 90 mmHg
B. 80 mmHg
C. 120 mmHg
D. Different pressures

**Answer: D.** Aortic and pulmonary closure occur when their respective arterial pressure exceeds ventricular pressure. The pulmonary and systemic pressure ranges differ; no single pressure applies to both.


### Q11 · source page 16

At what approximate ventricular pressure does the aortic valve open in the usual resting 120/80 mmHg example?

A. 80 mmHg
B. 8 mmHg
C. 90 mmHg
D. 12 mmHg

**Answer: A.** Opening begins when left-ventricular pressure slightly exceeds aortic diastolic pressure, approximately 80 mmHg in this example. This is a pressure crossover, not a fixed valve threshold.


### Q3 · source page 18

In the ventricles, plateau duration:

A. Is identical in all regions
B. Is longer in the subendocardial muscular layer
C. Is longer in the subepicardial muscular layer
D. None of the above

**Answer: B.** The standard transmural teaching model gives epicardial cells shorter action potentials than endocardial cells, helping explain epicardial-first repolarization despite endocardial-first activation.
Real regional action-potential durations vary, including midmyocardial cells; this is the intended simplified comparison.

### Q1 · source page 19

Sympathetic stimulation of the heart will:

A. Increase stroke volume
B. Increase heart rate
C. Increase ventricular contractile force
D. All of the above

**Answer: D.** The conventional physiological response is positive chronotropy and inotropy, usually with increased stroke volume when filling is adequate.
Stroke volume is load/rate dependent; the original 'definitely' is an overstatement at extreme tachycardia.
