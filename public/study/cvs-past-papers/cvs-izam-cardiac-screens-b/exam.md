## Questions

### Q15 · source page 2

In a normal heart, the atrial depolarization wave in aVR is usually:

A. Negative
B. Not recorded
C. Positive
D. Absent

**Answer: A.** Normal atrial depolarization is directed away from the right-shoulder positive electrode of aVR, producing a negative P wave.


### Q12 · source page 4

The atrial repolarization wave is:

A. The P wave
B. Always visible in the normal ECG
C. Usually not separately visible in the normal ECG
D. A positive wave in lead II

**Answer: C.** Atrial repolarization (Ta) is small and is normally obscured by the much larger QRS complex. P represents atrial depolarization.


### Q8 · source page 7

The Frank–Starling law:

A. Works only when diastolic pressure is below 160 mmHg
B. Works when mean blood pressure is between 60 and 160 mmHg
C. Works only when systolic pressure is below 160 mmHg
D. None of the above

**Answer: D.** Frank–Starling describes how greater ventricular filling increases developed force/stroke volume within the physiological range. A systemic-pressure interval of 60–160 mmHg describes a common autoregulation teaching range, not this law.


### Q7 · source page 8

In slow cardiac fibers, the resting/maximal diastolic potential:

A. None of the above
B. Implies an impulse rate always 70/sec
C. Is −90 mV
D. Is more positive than in fast fibers

**Answer: D.** Nodal pacemaker cells have a less negative maximum diastolic potential (roughly −60 mV) and no stable resting potential. Working ventricular myocytes rest near −90 mV.


### Q6 · source page 9

Plateau duration is longest among these in:

A. Pacemaker cells
B. Subepicardial muscle
C. Atrial muscle
D. Subendocardial ventricular muscle

**Answer: D.** Compared with the listed atrial and epicardial cells, endocardial ventricular cells have a longer plateau in the conventional course model. Nodal pacemaker cells lack a prominent ventricular-type plateau.
Regional heterogeneity exists; this comparison does not establish one maximum for every cell throughout the heart.

### Q5 · source page 10

Hyperkalemia typically:

A. Has a negative inotropic effect
B. Increases cardiac contractility
C. Has the same effect as hypercalcemia
D. Increases intracellular calcium release

**Answer: A.** Sufficient extracellular potassium elevation depolarizes the membrane, reduces available fast sodium channels and impairs excitation/conduction, tending to weaken contraction. Severe hyperkalemia can arrest the heart.


### Q3 · source page 13

Which listed channel is ligand-gated?

A. Dihydropyridine (L-type calcium) channel
B. Inward-rectifier potassium channel
C. Ryanodine receptor channel
D. Transient (T-type) calcium channel

**Answer: C.** Cardiac RyR2 is activated by cytosolic calcium, the ligand that triggers SR calcium release. DHP/L-type and T-type calcium channels are voltage-gated.


### Q2 · source page 14

During the ejection phase of systole:

A. Ventricles stop contracting during rapid ejection
B. Ventricular relaxation begins during reduced ejection
C. All of the above are incorrect
D. Ventricles contract continuously throughout

**Answer: B.** During reduced ejection, active tension begins falling and repolarization/relaxation begins, although forward flow continues briefly. Isovolumetric relaxation follows semilunar closure.


### Q1 · source page 15

In the usual ventricular activation/recovery pattern:

A. Depolarization and repolarization start at the same points
B. Depolarization and repolarization end at the same points
C. Repolarization starts subendocardially
D. Repolarization starts subepicardially

**Answer: D.** Epicardial action potentials are shorter in the standard model, so repolarization proceeds generally epicardium-to-endocardium despite the opposite transmural activation sequence.


### Q17 · source page 17

Between S1 and S2 there is:

A. None of the above
B. Isovolumetric contraction, ejection and isovolumetric relaxation
C. Isovolumetric contraction and ejection
D. Diastole

**Answer: C.** S1 marks AV closure/start of ventricular systole, and S2 marks semilunar closure. Isovolumetric relaxation begins after S2.


## Withheld source items

These are not silently assigned a default answer. They remain in the local original; the reason for exclusion is explicit.

- PDF page 1: Dicrotic notch is aortic valve closure/recoil, not coronary filling; none of the listed options accurately defines it.
- PDF page 3: Right and left ejection fractions are not necessarily equal and neither is universally larger.
- PDF page 5: Preload is variously expressed as end-diastolic volume or pressure; multiple offered proxies can fit.
- PDF page 6: Aortic closure is a pressure crossover, not a universal fixed 5/80/120/90 mmHg.
- PDF page 11: No question: status screen.
- PDF page 12: SA-node inactivation can cause an escape rhythm or a pause; both rhythm timing and functional consequences vary.
- PDF page 16: The numeric typical cardiac index and the statement that cardiac index varies can both be true; not a clean single-answer item.
