## Questions

### Q1 · source page 1

What is the role of potassium channels in fast cardiac fibers?

A. Induction of threshold potential
B. Induction of repolarization
C. Both depolarization and repolarization
D. None of the above

**Answer: B.** Outward potassium currents restore the membrane toward a negative potential, especially in phase 3. The rapid phase-0 upstroke is carried mainly by sodium.


### Q2 · source page 2

Calcium current is responsible for:

A. Slow-fiber depolarization
B. Slow-fiber repolarization
C. Fast-fiber phase-0 depolarization
D. Fast-fiber repolarization

**Answer: A.** In nodal slow-response tissue, L-type calcium influx produces phase 0. Potassium efflux repolarizes; fast working-myocyte phase 0 is sodium-mediated.


### Q3 · source page 3

In normal limb leads, P and the predominant QRS deflection are usually:

A. In the same direction/polarity
B. In opposite directions/polarities
C. Unpredictable
D. Determined by the S-wave direction

**Answer: A.** Normal atrial and ventricular mean depolarization vectors both point generally leftward and inferiorly, so their main deflections commonly share polarity.
The source calls the ventricular deflection 'R'; an R wave is positive by definition. Interpret this as predominant QRS polarity; aVR and equiphasic leads require care.

### Q6 · source page 6

Which contains slow-response cardiac fibers?

A. Atrial working muscle
B. AV node
C. Ventricular working muscle
D. Purkinje fibers

**Answer: B.** The AV node uses a calcium-dependent slow upstroke. Atrial/ventricular working cells and Purkinje fibers have fast sodium-dependent phase 0.


### Q7 · source page 7

The atrioventricular valves close:

A. After isovolumetric contraction
B. After isovolumetric relaxation
C. Just before isovolumetric contraction
D. Just before isovolumetric relaxation

**Answer: C.** AV closure is the boundary starting isovolumetric contraction; semilunar valves remain closed until ejection begins.


### Q8 · source page 8

When heart rate increases:

A. Only systole shortens
B. Only diastole shortens
C. Systole shortens more than diastole
D. Diastole shortens more than systole

**Answer: D.** Both phases can shorten, but diastole loses proportionately more time. Very fast rates compromise filling and coronary perfusion time.


### Q9 · source page 9

Which has the fastest impulse conduction?

A. Internodal atrial fibers
B. His bundle
C. Purkinje fibers
D. Ventricular working muscle

**Answer: C.** The Purkinje network conducts very rapidly to synchronize ventricular activation; AV nodal tissue is the slowest important normal conduction segment.


### Q11 · source page 11

A typical normal left-ventricular ejection fraction is:

A. 80%
B. 60%
C. 60 mL
D. 80 mL

**Answer: B.** Ejection fraction is SV/EDV, a dimensionless fraction commonly about 60%. A volume in mL is not an ejection fraction.


### Q12 · source page 12

The sympathetic system:

A. Increases atrial contraction force
B. Increases AV delay
C. Increases plateau duration
D. All of the above

**Answer: A.** β1 stimulation increases atrial contractile force and usually accelerates AV conduction, shortening delay. A universal plateau prolongation is not the defining response.


### Q13 · source page 13

During the ejection phase:

A. Ventricles are always actively contracting
B. Ventricles are always relaxed
C. Blood flow stops for a while
D. Forward outflow continues during the phase

**Answer: D.** Ejection denotes forward flow through open semilunar valves; active ventricular tension begins declining during reduced ejection before outflow ends.


### Q15 · source page 15

Ventricular repolarization generally starts in:

A. Subendocardial muscle
B. Subepicardial muscle
C. The same site where depolarization starts
D. None of the above

**Answer: B.** Shorter epicardial action potentials permit earlier epicardial recovery even though endocardial activation occurs first in the simplified transmural model.
