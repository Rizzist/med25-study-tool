## Questions

### Q1 · source page 1

If a flow of 10 mL/sec passes through a vessel with an area of 2 cm², what is the blood velocity?

A. 2 cm/sec
B. 10 cm/sec
C. 5 cm/sec
D. 1 cm/sec

**Answer: C.** Mean velocity = volume flow / cross-sectional area = 10/2 = 5 cm/sec. Units reduce from cm³/sec divided by cm² to cm/sec.


### Q2 · source page 2

Chemoreceptor signals activate when blood pressure reaches which level?

A. Less than 100 mmHg
B. Less than 50 mmHg
C. Less than 30 mmHg
D. Less than 80 mmHg

**Answer: D.** In the Guyton low-perfusion model, peripheral chemoreceptor cardiovascular reflexes become especially important below about 80 mmHg. Reduced carotid/aortic-body perfusion lowers oxygen and raises CO₂/H⁺.
A textbook approximate threshold for the circulatory chemoreflex, not an on/off blood-pressure threshold for all chemoreceptor activity.

### Q3 · source page 3

Turbulent blood flow is more likely if:

A. Vessel diameter decreases
B. Blood viscosity increases
C. Blood velocity increases
D. Blood density decreases

**Answer: C.** Reynolds number rises with velocity, diameter and density, and falls with viscosity. Increased velocity therefore favors turbulence.


### Q4 · source page 4

Which is a sign of short-term control of blood flow?

A. Angiogenesis
B. Active hyperemia
C. Decrease in PO₂
D. Renin–angiotensin system

**Answer: B.** Active hyperemia is the immediate increase in flow accompanying increased tissue activity. Angiogenesis is structural long-term adaptation; low oxygen is a stimulus rather than the named flow response.


### Q5 · source page 5

Increasing arteriolar diameter in a muscle vascular bed causes:

A. A decrease in capillary hydrostatic pressure
B. None of the above
C. An increase in lymph flow
D. A decrease in capillary filtration

**Answer: C.** Lower precapillary resistance transmits more upstream pressure to capillaries, increasing filtration. Increased interstitial fluid formation raises lymph return while drainage capacity remains adequate.


### Q6 · source page 6

A healthy young woman moves from supine to standing. Which compensatory pattern is correct?

A. Decreased plasma renin; increased renal blood flow; decreased total vascular resistance
B. Increased plasma renin; decreased renal blood flow; increased total vascular resistance
C. Decreased plasma renin; decreased renal blood flow; decreased total vascular resistance
D. Increased plasma renin; increased renal blood flow; increased total vascular resistance

**Answer: B.** Orthostatic pooling reduces central filling. Baroreflex sympathetic activation raises vascular resistance and can lower renal perfusion; renin rises as renal sympathetic/low-perfusion signals develop.
The time courses differ: neural compensation is rapid, whereas the hormonal response develops later.

### Q7 · source page 7

Which factor does NOT increase lymph flow?

A. Increased capillary permeability
B. Decreased plasma colloid osmotic pressure
C. Elevated capillary pressure
D. Decreased interstitial-fluid colloid osmotic pressure

**Answer: D.** Reducing interstitial oncotic pressure reduces the outward oncotic driving force in the conventional Starling model. The other choices increase fluid/protein escape and lymph formation.


### Q8 · source page 8

Which change occurs with aortic regurgitation?

A. Aortic compliance increases
B. The aortic notch disappears
C. All of the above
D. Pulse pressure decreases

**Answer: B.** An incompetent aortic valve blunts or removes the normal closure-related incisura. Regurgitation typically widens, rather than narrows, pulse pressure; it does not require increased aortic compliance.


### Q9 · source page 9

Which reflects an active role of endothelium?

A. Production of nitric oxide
B. Production of potassium ions
C. Production of hydrogen ions
D. Production of acetate ions

**Answer: A.** Endothelial nitric oxide is synthesized by endothelial NO synthase and relaxes adjacent vascular smooth muscle via soluble guanylyl cyclase/cGMP.


### Q10 · source page 10

What is the main regulator of blood flow in active skeletal muscle?

A. Metabolism
B. Sympathetic innervation
C. Vasopressin
D. Oncotic pressure in muscle capillaries

**Answer: A.** Local metabolic demand produces vasodilation and matches muscle perfusion to activity. Sympathetic tone remains important systemically but is not the principal local matching signal.


### Q11 · source page 11

How does venous distensibility compare with arterial distensibility?

A. About 8 times greater
B. About 6 times greater
C. About 3 times greater
D. About 2 times greater

**Answer: A.** Guyton's approximate comparison gives veins about eight times the distensibility of corresponding arteries. Compliance additionally depends on starting volume, so it is not the same ratio.
An approximate textbook comparison, not a fixed ratio for every vessel.

### Q13 · source page 13

Which finding can result from heart failure?

A. Decreased central venous pressure
B. Distended neck veins
C. Increased plasma potassium
D. Increased extracellular oncotic pressure

**Answer: B.** Systemic venous congestion, particularly with right-sided failure, raises jugular venous pressure. Hyperkalemia depends on renal function and treatment and is not the defining consequence.


### Q14 · source page 14

Which contains the greatest volume of blood?

A. Capillaries
B. Veins
C. Heart
D. Arteries

**Answer: B.** Systemic veins and venules are the major capacitance reservoir and contain the largest share of circulating blood at rest.


### Q15 · source page 15

Which factor increases coronary arterial resistance?

A. Endothelin
B. Adenosine
C. Prostacyclin
D. Acetylcholine

**Answer: A.** Endothelin is a potent vasoconstrictor. Adenosine and prostacyclin dilate; acetylcholine normally promotes endothelial NO-mediated dilation when endothelium is intact.
Acetylcholine can constrict when endothelial function is impaired; the normal intact-endothelium assumption is intended.

### Q16 · source page 16

Which are termed nutritional vessels and contain approximately the stated percentage of blood?

A. Arteries — 13%
B. Pulmonary vessels — 9%
C. Capillaries — 7%
D. Veins — 27%

**Answer: C.** Capillaries are the principal exchange vessels. The course's simplified distribution pairs them with approximately 7%; the precise fraction varies with whether small arterioles are grouped with the microcirculation.
The key is capillary exchange function. Do not treat 7% as an exact isolated capillary volume in every classification.

### Q17 · source page 17

How does sympathetic inhibition affect the pressure–volume curves of arteries and veins?

A. No relationship
B. More pressure is generated at a lower volume
C. Both shift to the right
D. Both shift to the left

**Answer: C.** Relaxation allows a greater volume at a given pressure: the pressure–volume relation shifts rightward (or downward when pressure is plotted vertically).


### Q19 · source page 19

Hemorrhage triggers all the following compensatory reactions EXCEPT:

A. Increased catecholamine secretion
B. Bradycardia
C. Venous vasoconstriction
D. Arterial vasoconstriction

**Answer: B.** The usual acute baroreflex response includes tachycardia, sympathetic arteriolar and venous constriction, and catecholamine release.
Paradoxical bradycardia can occur in special/severe hemorrhagic settings; the question asks for the usual compensatory pattern.

### Q20 · source page 20

A 30-year-old has taken a high dose of furosemide. Which listed treatment best addresses the expected fluid loss?

A. Plasma injection
B. Blood transfusion
C. Normal saline with electrolytes
D. Adrenaline injection

**Answer: C.** For the question's implied diuretic-induced volume/electrolyte depletion, replacement fluid and monitored correction of electrolytes address the mechanism; blood/plasma/adrenaline do not replace the specific losses.
Educational best choice only. Actual treatment depends on volume status, renal function, electrolytes and the reason furosemide was prescribed; saline is not appropriate automatically after every large dose.

### Q21 · source page 21

Reduced carotid sinus pressure causes a decrease in:

A. Heart rate
B. Myocardial contractility
C. Venous capacitance
D. Cardiac output

**Answer: C.** Reduced stretch reduces baroreceptor firing, increasing sympathetic output. Venoconstriction reduces capacitance; heart rate and contractility rise.


### Q22 · source page 22

Which has an inverse relationship with blood flow through a vessel?

A. Vascular resistance
B. Vessel diameter
C. Amount of elastic fibers in the vessel wall
D. Pressure difference across the vessel

**Answer: A.** At a given pressure gradient, flow = ΔP/R. Higher resistance means lower flow.


### Q23 · source page 23

During exercise in a healthy student, which pattern occurs in active muscle?

A. Increased arteriolar diameter; increased adenosine; increased blood flow
B. Decreased arteriolar diameter; increased adenosine; increased blood flow
C. Increased arteriolar diameter; decreased adenosine; decreased blood flow
D. Decreased arteriolar diameter; decreased adenosine; increased blood flow

**Answer: A.** Metabolite accumulation accompanies activity and contributes to arteriolar dilation and increased perfusion. Adenosine is one contributor, not the sole regulator.


### Q24 · source page 24

Which is an endothelial growth/angiogenesis factor?

A. EDRF
B. Nitric oxide
C. Angiogenin
D. Angiotensin

**Answer: C.** Angiogenin promotes new-vessel formation. EDRF/NO mainly names a rapid vasodilator signal rather than the listed angiogenic growth factor.


### Q25 · source page 25

Which tends to decrease cardiac output?

A. Myocardial infarction
B. Beriberi
C. Anemia
D. Increased thyroid activity

**Answer: A.** Myocardial injury can reduce pumping capacity. Beriberi, anemia and hyperthyroidism classically produce high-output states before secondary decompensation.


### Q26 · source page 26

A drug increases myocardial contractility. Which change is expected?

A. Central venous pressure and pulse pressure increase
B. Central venous pressure decreases and cardiac output increases
C. Central venous pressure and arterial compliance increase
D. Central venous pressure and mean arterial pressure decrease

**Answer: B.** A stronger cardiac function curve empties the venous side more effectively: at an unchanged venous-return curve, the equilibrium moves toward higher output and lower right-atrial pressure.
