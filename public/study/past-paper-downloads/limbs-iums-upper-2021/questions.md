### 15 · limbs-iums-upper-2021-q015

Which is not present in the scapular anastomosis?

A. Anterior circumflex humeral artery
B. Circumflex scapular artery
C. Subscapular artery
D. Acromial branch of the thoracoacromial trunk

Source: upper limb · original Q15, page 16.

Status: Ungraded — The supplied grid marks B, but circumflex scapular is a principal scapular anastomotic artery. The stem does not distinguish the scapular-body network from the acromial/perihumeral networks, making A versus D dependent on its intended scope. This defective source is retained ungraded; do not memorize B as absent.

### 16 · limbs-iums-upper-2021-q016

Damage to what peripheral nerve(s) would impair external pronation of forearm?

A. Anterior interosseous nerve
B. Radial nerve
C. Only the median nerve
D. Median and anterior interosseous nerves

Practice correction (original choices above): The intended pair D identifies pronator teres supply via median nerve and pronator quadratus via its AIN branch. Because the original says impair rather than abolish, an isolated AIN lesion or proximal median lesion can also impair pronation: A, C and D are accepted. 'External pronation' in the source is not a standard movement term.

Source: upper limb · original Q16, page 18.

### 18 · limbs-iums-upper-2021-q018

If intrinsic hand motor function and sensation in the fifth digit and ulnar half of the fourth digit are lost, where is the ulnar nerve damaged?

A. After Guyon's canal
B. In the carpal tunnel
C. At the elbow
D. Before Guyon's canal

Source: upper limb · original Q18, page 21.

Status: Ungraded — The supplied grid marks A, but the findings do not uniquely localize a lesion. A proximal ulnar lesion at the elbow or before Guyon's canal can cause both deficits; distal canal involvement depends on branching level and which sensory surfaces are affected. No forearm motor or dorsal-hand sensory findings are supplied. Retained ungraded rather than teaching a false unique level.

### 20 · limbs-iums-upper-2021-q020

A 10yr old fractures her humerus at surgical neck. What damage could not be checked?

A. Axillary nerve dysfunction
B. Sensory loss over the upper medial arm
C. Sensory loss over the upper lateral arm
D. Deltoid atrophy

Practice correction (original choices above): The axillary nerve supplies deltoid and the upper lateral arm patch, not upper medial arm skin. Deltoid atrophy is a later sign, not an immediate post-fracture finding. The source's 'could not be checked' wording is clarified to the nerve territory being tested.

Source: upper limb · original Q20, page 20.

Practice correction (original choices above): Teres minor is a lateral rotator. The other choices medially rotate. Infraspinatus also laterally rotates but is not offered: teres minor is not the only lateral rotator in the body.
