## Answer key and provenance

### 1 · D1

Key: D — Carbohydrates, fats, and proteins

Key provenance: Editorial study answer / source review note; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Carbohydrate, fat and protein supply metabolic energy. Vitamins, minerals and water do not supply calories.

Source: Original Q1; page 1.



### 2 · D2

Key: D — Ethnic heritage or regional cuisine

Key provenance: Editorial study answer / source review note; not an official university key.

Source: Original Q2; page 1.



### 3 · D3

Key: A — Most likely low in fat

Key provenance: Editorial study answer / source review note; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Among the original options, low in fat is the best general comparison for these foods without large additions of fat. Preparation and the rest of the diet matter.

Source: Original Q3; page 2.



### 4 · D4

Key: A — Starch, glycogen, and fiber

Key provenance: Editorial study answer / source review note; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Starch, glycogen and many dietary fibers are polysaccharides. 'Fiber' is a broader functional category and also includes lignin, which is not a polysaccharide.

Source: Original Q4; page 2.



### 5 · D5

Key: B — Protein

Key provenance: Editorial study answer / source review note; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Protein is the intended body-fuel answer once other reserves become severely depleted. The source wording oversimplifies timing: some protein catabolism and gluconeogenesis already occur before all fat has been exhausted.

Source: Original Q5; page 3.



### 6 · D6

Key: D — Maintenance of heartbeat and body temperature

Key provenance: Editorial study answer / source review note; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Basal metabolism supports maintenance functions at rest, including cardiac work and thermoregulation.

Source: Original Q6; page 3.



### 7 · D7

Key: A — Basal metabolism

Key provenance: Editorial study answer / source review note; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Basal/resting expenditure is usually largest in sedentary or moderately active adults. Extreme activity can change the proportions.

Source: Original Q7; page 4.



### 8 · D8

Key: A — Body mass index

Key provenance: Editorial study answer / source review note; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: BMI is weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared. It does not directly measure fat distribution or muscle mass.

Source: Original Q8; page 4.



### 9 · D9

Key: C — Replacing liquid oils with solid fats whenever possible

Key provenance: Editorial study answer / source review note; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: The recommendation is generally the reverse: replace suitable sources of saturated/solid fats with unsaturated oils, considering the whole eating pattern.

Source: Original Q9; page 4.



### 10 · D10
