## Answer key and provenance

### 1 · tums-feb-2024-q001

Key: C — 50–70

Key provenance: Editorial study answer / source review note; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Basal/resting energy expenditure commonly accounts for roughly 60–70% of total energy expenditure in sedentary adults; the fraction varies with activity and illness.

Source: Original Q1; page 2.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 166–169 (PDF counter).

### 2 · tums-feb-2024-q002

Key: B — Decreased

Key provenance: Editorial study answer / source review note; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: REE tends to fall with age, importantly through loss of metabolically active lean tissue.

Source: Original Q2; page 2.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 166–169 (PDF counter).

### 3 · tums-feb-2024-q003

Key: B — Non-exercise activity thermogenesis

Key provenance: Editorial study answer / source review note; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Non-exercise activity thermogenesis refers to energy expenditure from daily movement rather than meal processing; the other terms describe TEF.

Source: Original Q3; page 2.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 166–169 (PDF counter).

### 4 · tums-feb-2024-q004

Key: A — In healthy weight person BMI is 18.5–24.9

Key provenance: Editorial study answer / source review note; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: The standard adult healthy-weight BMI range is 18.5–24.9 kg/m²; 25–29.9 is overweight and ≥30 is obesity.

Source: Original Q4; page 2.

https://www.cdc.gov/bmi/adult-calculator/bmi-categories.html

### 5 · tums-feb-2024-q005

Key: C — Increase and decrease respectively

Key provenance: Editorial study answer / source review note; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Older adults often benefit from a higher protein intake per kilogram while energy expenditure tends to fall. This is a general healthy-ageing teaching point; kidney disease and other illness require individual assessment.

Source: Original Q5; page 2.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 384–395 (PDF counter).
