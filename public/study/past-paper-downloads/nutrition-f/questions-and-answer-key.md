## Answer key and provenance

### 1 · F1

Key: D — 27 mg

Key provenance: Editorial study answer / source review note; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: 27 mg/day is the pregnancy RDA in the DRI framework, not a treatment dose.

Source: Original Q1; page 1.



### 2 · F2

Key: D — 9 kcal

Key provenance: Editorial study answer / source review note; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: The general physiological energy factor for fat is 9 kcal/g.

Source: Original Q2; page 2.



### 3 · F3

Key: A — Chromium

Key provenance: Editorial study answer / source review note; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Chromium is the historical textbook answer. Current evidence leaves its essentiality and physiological role uncertain; this question does not establish a benefit from chromium supplements.

Source: Original Q3; page 3.



### 4 · F4

Key: B — 3 to 9

Key provenance: Editorial study answer / source review note; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: The stated FAO/WHO convention assigns degrees of polymerization 3-9 to oligosaccharides. Other texts use slightly different boundaries. The source's marked 2-5 range is not this convention; the study version names the convention and repairs the option.

Source: Original Q4; page 4.

https://www.fao.org/4/w8079e/w8079e07.htm

### 5 · F5

Key: D — Lactose

Key provenance: Editorial study answer / source review note; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Lactose is a disaccharide composed of glucose and galactose.

Source: Original Q5; page 5.



### 6 · F6

Key: C — Selenium

Key provenance: Editorial study answer / source review note; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: The intended antioxidant partnership is selenium-dependent glutathione peroxidase with vitamin E. Peroxidases remove peroxides; vitamin E limits lipid-peroxidation propagation.

Source: Original Q6; page 6.



### 7 · F7

Key: C — Retinal

Key provenance: Editorial study answer / source review note; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Retinal, specifically 11-cis retinal in the dark-adapted visual pigment, is the chromophore used in phototransduction.

Source: Original Q7; page 7.



### 8 · F8

Key: B — 10%

Key provenance: Editorial study answer / source review note; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: About 10% is the mixed-diet teaching approximation. TEF varies with diet composition and is not a universal fixed fraction.

Source: Original Q8; page 8.



### 9 · F9

Key: B — Vitamin D

Key provenance: Editorial study answer / source review note; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Vitamin D3 is produced through UVB-dependent conversion of 7-dehydrocholesterol in skin.

Source: Original Q9; page 9.



### 10 · F10

Key: B — Vitamin E

Key provenance: Editorial study answer / source review note; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Vitamin E is the best answer for the major lipid-phase, chain-breaking vitamin antioxidant among these choices. This is not a claim that no other nutrient can participate in antioxidant defenses.

Source: Original Q10; page 10.

### 21 · F21

Key: B — 1-alpha-hydroxylation

Key provenance: Editorial study answer / source review note; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: The activating renal step adds a hydroxyl group at carbon 1-alpha to 25(OH)D. The liver performs the main 25-hydroxylation. Renal 24-hydroxylation also occurs but is a different, largely inactivating pathway; naming activation removes that ambiguity.
