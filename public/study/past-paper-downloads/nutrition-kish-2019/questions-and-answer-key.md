## Answer key and provenance

### 1 · kish-2019-q001

Key: D — 1.7

Key provenance: Source mark with editorial review; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: 1.7 is the conventional comparison intended here. Relative sweetness is not a fixed physical constant: concentration, temperature and the testing method alter it.

Source: Original Q1; page 1.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 57–58.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2758952/

### 2 · kish-2019-q002

Key: C — Phenylalanine/Aspartic acid

Key provenance: Source mark with editorial review; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Aspartame is the methyl ester of a dipeptide containing aspartic acid and phenylalanine. The amino-acid pair is C.

Source: Original Q2; page 1.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 67–68.

### 3 · kish-2019-q003

Key: D — Dietary fiber

Key provenance: Source mark with editorial review; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: D is the intended dietary inhibitor among these choices. Phytate and other compounds in some fiber-rich foods inhibit nonheme-iron absorption; it is not true that every purified fiber has this effect. Vitamin C and gastric acidity favor absorption.

Source: Original Q3; page 1.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 261–265.
https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iron-HealthProfessional/

### 4 · kish-2019-q004

Key: B — Increasing the clotting/Increasing the chemotaxy of neutrophils

Key provenance: Source mark with editorial review; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Thromboxane A2 promotes platelet aggregation; leukotriene B4 is a neutrophil chemoattractant. Different leukotrienes also have other actions, so LT here refers principally to LTB4.

Source: Original Q4; page 1.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 88–89.

### 5 · kish-2019-q005

Key: C — 25

Key provenance: Source mark with editorial review; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: About 25% is the closest conventional adult estimate among these choices. Fractional absorption varies with dose, age, vitamin D status and physiologic demand; it is not universally 25%.

Source: Original Q5; page 1.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 254–257.
https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Calcium-HealthProfessional/

### 6 · kish-2019-q006

Key: A — Increasing the blood calcium

Key provenance: Source mark with editorial review; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Parathyroid hormone raises circulating calcium through renal calcium retention, calcitriol-mediated intestinal absorption and, when needed, mobilization from bone.

Source: Original Q6; page 1.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 254–257.

### 7 · kish-2019-q007

Key: B — Selenium deficiency

Key provenance: Source mark with editorial review; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Keshan disease is an endemic cardiomyopathy associated with selenium deficiency, interacting with other environmental or infectious factors.

Source: Original Q7; page 1.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF p. 267.
https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Selenium-HealthProfessional/

### 8 · kish-2019-q008

Key: A — Transamination

Key provenance: Source mark with editorial review; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Pyridoxal phosphate is the coenzyme for aminotransferases. B6 participates in many amino-acid reactions, but transamination is the best listed answer.

Source: Original Q8; page 2.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 228–229.

### 9 · kish-2019-q009

Key: A — Deficiency of thiamine

Key provenance: Source mark with editorial review; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Beriberi is caused by thiamine deficiency; dry and wet forms emphasize neurologic and cardiovascular manifestations respectively.

Source: Original Q9; page 2.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 225–226.

### 10 · kish-2019-q010

Key: A — Deficiency of Niacin

Key provenance: Source mark with editorial review; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Pellagra results from inadequate niacin availability, including inadequate tryptophan contribution, and classically causes dermatitis, diarrhea and dementia.

Source: Original Q10; page 2.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 227–228.

### 11 · kish-2019-q011

Key: C — Riboflavin deficiency

Key provenance: Source mark with editorial review; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Riboflavin deficiency can cause ocular symptoms including sensitivity to light. It is the best of the nutritional options, not the main cause of photophobia in clinical practice overall.

Source: Original Q11; page 2.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 226–227.

### 12 · kish-2019-q012

Key: D — Ileum

Key provenance: Source mark with editorial review; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: The intrinsic-factor–B12 complex is absorbed by receptor-mediated uptake in the distal ileum; the stomach supplies intrinsic factor but is not the uptake site.

Source: Original Q12; page 2.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 230–232.
https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB12-HealthProfessional/

### 13 · kish-2019-q013

Key: D — Megaloblastic Anemia

Key provenance: Source mark with editorial review; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Either deficiency can impair DNA synthesis and produce megaloblastic anemia. B12 deficiency also causes neurologic injury, which folate alone does not prevent.

Source: Original Q13; page 2.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 230–232.

### 14 · kish-2019-q014

Key: D — Vitamin D / Vitamin A

Key provenance: Source mark with editorial review; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Calcitriol and retinoic acid act through nuclear receptors to regulate gene expression, explaining the hormone-like description of vitamins D and A.

Source: Original Q14; page 2.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 212–221.

### 15 · kish-2019-q015

Source: Original Q15; page 2.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 109–113.

### 16 · kish-2019-q016

Key: B — Retinoic Acid

Key provenance: Editorial study answer / source review note; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Retinoic acid regulates gene expression and differentiation. Its effects on proliferation are context-dependent; it is not a universal proliferation stimulant.

Source: Original Q16; page 2.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 212–215.
https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminA-HealthProfessional/

### 17 · kish-2019-q017

Key: C — 1-2 year

Key provenance: Source mark with editorial review; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: The supplied key selects 1–2 years, a common period of presentation during rapid early growth. Nutritional rickets also occurs in younger infants and adolescents; there is no universal prevalence peak for every population.

Source: Original Q17; page 3.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 218–221.
https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241516587

### 18 · kish-2019-q018

Key: B — Dietary fiber (pectin)

Key provenance: Source mark with editorial review; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Viscous soluble fiber such as pectin can lower LDL cholesterol. Omega-3 fatty acids primarily lower triglycerides; exercise remains beneficial but is not the most direct cholesterol-lowering mechanism offered.

Source: Original Q18; page 3.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 69–74, 576–585.

Source: Original Q19; page 3.

### 20 · kish-2019-q020

Key: A — Iron

Key provenance: Editorial study answer / source review note; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Unnecessary iron in an iron-replete person can cause harm; iron overload promotes tissue injury. Cancer or cardiovascular disease alone is not a blanket reason to withhold medically indicated iron. The study wording specifies excess iron without deficiency.

Source: Original Q20; page 3.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 263–265.
https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iron-HealthProfessional/

### 21 · kish-2019-q021

Key: C — Cabbages

Key provenance: Editorial study answer / source review note; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Cabbage supplies vitamin K. Warfarin users should keep vitamin K intake consistent rather than automatically avoid all vitamin-K-containing foods. The study wording corrects that unsafe blanket advice.

Source: Original Q21; page 3.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 448–449.
https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminK-HealthProfessional/
