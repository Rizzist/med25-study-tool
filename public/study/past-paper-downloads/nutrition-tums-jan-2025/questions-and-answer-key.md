## Answer key and provenance

### 1 · tums-jan-2025-q001

Key: A — The amount of calories present in each gram of food

Key provenance: Source mark with editorial review; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Energy density expresses food energy per unit mass, usually kcal/g, rather than the amount of micronutrients.

Source: Original Q1; page 1.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 29–30, 203.

### 2 · tums-jan-2025-q002

Key: D — Exclusion of food groups

Key provenance: Source mark with editorial review; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: A balanced, adequate and moderate diet is planned around variety. Exclusion of whole groups is not a universal principle, although specific clinical conditions may require substitutions.

Source: Original Q2; page 1.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 35–40.

### 3 · tums-jan-2025-q003

Key: B — Fruits, vegetables, whole grains, meat and fish, dairy

Key provenance: Source mark with editorial review; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: B is the closest representation of fruit, vegetables, grains, protein foods and dairy in the course's five-group scheme. The protein-food group also includes plant proteins; it is not restricted to meat and fish.

Source: Original Q3; page 1.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 36–40.
https://www.myplate.gov/sites/default/files/2020-12/MyPlate_style_guide_2020-2025.pdf

### 4 · tums-jan-2025-q004

Key: C — The speed of glucose absorption after eating and the increase in blood sugar

Key provenance: Source mark with editorial review; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: The glycemic response is the change in blood glucose after food intake. The course option also describes the rate of appearance/absorption underlying that response.

Source: Original Q4; page 1.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 70–74.

### 5 · tums-jan-2025-q005

Key: A — At least 50 grams

Key provenance: Editorial study answer / source review note; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: The source marks 50 g as a traditional minimum protein-sparing teaching estimate. This is not the general adult carbohydrate RDA, which is 130 g/day, and the effect depends on total energy intake. The study version explicitly asks for the source's conventional estimate.

Source: Original Q5; page 2.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 73–74, 121.

### 6 · tums-jan-2025-q006

Key: C — Nerve cells and red blood cells

Key provenance: Editorial study answer / source review note; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Red blood cells lack mitochondria and require glycolysis. Neural tissue mainly uses glucose in the usual fed state but can use ketones during prolonged fasting; the word exclusively was inaccurate for the combined option.

Source: Original Q6; page 2.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 57–60, 158–164.

### 7 · tums-jan-2025-q007

Key: D — Chain length and saturation

Key provenance: Source mark with editorial review; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Chain length and the number/configuration of double bonds strongly determine physical and metabolic properties. Temperature also affects a sample's physical state, but D is the intrinsic structural determinant being tested.

Source: Original Q7; page 2.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 83–89.

### 8 · tums-jan-2025-q008

Key: B — The position of the nearest double bond to the methyl (CH3) end of the carbon chain

Key provenance: Source mark with editorial review; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Omega numbering locates the first double bond counted from the terminal methyl end, not the carboxyl carbon.

Source: Original Q8; page 2.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 85–89.

### 9 · tums-jan-2025-q009

Key: B — These drugs simultaneously inhibit COX-1 and COX-2, with COX-1 playing a role in producing gastric mucin

Key provenance: Source mark with editorial review; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Nonselective NSAIDs inhibit cyclooxygenase and reduce protective prostaglandins, impairing gastric mucus/bicarbonate defense and mucosal blood flow. COX-1 does not directly manufacture mucin.

Source: Original Q9; page 3.

https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/peptic-ulcers-stomach-ulcers/symptoms-causes
DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 88–89.

### 10 · tums-jan-2025-q010

Key: C — 82 Kcal

Key provenance: Source mark with editorial review; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Using mass percentages: protein 6.5 g × 4 = 26 kcal; fat 6 g × 9 = 54 kcal; carbohydrate 0.5 g × 4 = 2 kcal. Total = 82 kcal.

Source: Original Q10; page 3.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 29–30.

### 11 · tums-jan-2025-q011

Key: D — It shows that the person has increased intake of calories or carbohydrates and is storing fat

Key provenance: Source mark with editorial review; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: In a steady nutritional context, an RQ above 1 can reflect net lipogenesis during carbohydrate overfeeding. A respiratory exchange ratio measured during intense exercise may exceed 1 for other reasons.

Source: Original Q11; page 3.

Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed., chapter 73, respiratory quotient and conversion of carbohydrate into fat.
DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 158–163.

### 12 · tums-jan-2025-q012

Key: C — AT

Key provenance: Source mark with editorial review; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Activity thermogenesis varies most across activity patterns; basal metabolic rate and the thermic effect of food are less variable. TEE is the total, not a component.

Source: Original Q12; page 4.

DeBruyne, Pinna & Whitney, Nutrition & Diet Therapy, 9th ed. (2016), PDF pp. 161–165.

### 13 · tums-jan-2025-q013

Key: C — Albumin

Key provenance: Source mark with editorial review; not an official university key.

Existing answer note: Albumin tends to decrease during inflammation; ferritin, fibrinogen and ceruloplasmin are positive acute-phase proteins. Albumin alone is not a nutrition-status marker.
